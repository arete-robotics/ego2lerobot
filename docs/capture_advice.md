# Capture advice: chest-mounted action camera

How to film hand work so that the hands can be labelled well. Written for a chest-mounted ultra-wide action camera;
most of it also applies to head mounts and phones.

## Before the real session
Record a **one-minute test** in the real room with the real task and send it first. We return the overlay preview
and the QC report, and you can fix the mount or the light before recording hours.

## Mounting
- **Chest harness, camera on the sternum**, a hand's width below the collarbones, strapped tight so it does not
  swing or sag when you bend.
- **Tilt it down** so that your hands at arm's length sit in the upper half of the frame and your hands at the belly
  are still in view. A typical angle is 30–45° below horizontal. Check by holding both arms straight out, then
  bringing both hands to your stomach: both should stay in frame.
- Keep straps, lanyards, jacket flaps, hair and long sleeves away from the lens.

## Camera settings
| Setting | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lens / FOV mode | the widest mode, **distortion correction ("dewarp", "linear") off** | Keeps hands at the edges in view; we model the lens instead of letting the camera bend the image |
| Stabilisation | **off** (also horizon levelling) | Electronic stabilisation crops, shifts and rotates every frame differently, which breaks the 3D geometry |
| Resolution | 1080p or higher; 4K is fine | We label at about 1000 px wide, so 4K adds file size but not accuracy |
| Frame rate | 30 fps; 60 fps if the room is bright | 60 fps gives sharper fast hands only when there is enough light; we reduce to 30 fps anyway |
| Same settings | for every recording, and tell us the camera model and mode | The lens model depends on the mode; mixed modes in one delivery get mixed 3D errors |

**Calibration clip (once per camera and mode, 30 seconds):** film a printed checkerboard (for example 9×6 squares on
A4, taped flat) while moving it through the whole frame, including the corners and edges. This lets us measure the
lens instead of using a nominal value, which improves hand distances.

## Light
- Bright, even light. Turn the room lights on, open curtains, but do not face a bright window: hands in front of a
  window turn into silhouettes.
- In bathrooms, wipe the lens if it fogs or gets splashed.

## Hands
- Keep **both hands in front of the chest** and in view as much as the task allows. Hands above the shoulders, behind
  the body or below the waist are out of frame.
- **Gloves lower detection.** If gloves are needed, use tight-fitting ones in a plain colour that stands out from the
  surfaces (not white gloves on white sheets or tiles, not skin-coloured).
- Mirrors and glossy surfaces can show a second pair of hands; avoid working face-on to a mirror where you can.

## Clips and files
- Continuous recordings of 5–20 minutes are fine. We cut them into episodes of 3–15 seconds automatically.
- **One task per file** where possible (for example *make bed*, *clean sink*), and say the task in the file name.
- Short breaks are fine; pauses longer than a few seconds without hands in view become gaps between episodes.
- Send the **original files from the camera's card**. Phone apps and messengers re-encode and shrink video.

## What to avoid
- Stabilisation or dewarp on; changing modes between recordings.
- A loose mount that points at the floor or the ceiling when you bend.
- Dim rooms with fast hand motion (blur), and strong backlight.
- Other people's faces and personal documents in view.
